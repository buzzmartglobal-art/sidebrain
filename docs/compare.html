Sider vs Monica vs Sidebrain: which AI sidebar actually saves you money

They all do the same visible trick: a panel next to the page. They differ on one thing that decides the bill — who buys the tokens — and on a second thing you only notice a year later: where the history lives.

Short answer: if you already pay for ChatGPT, Claude or a Gemini key, Sidebrain can spend that quota instead of asking you to buy a second copy of it. If you want something you install and forget, it is also the weakest of these five today — it is beta, macOS-only, needs an extension installed, and the Pro features need a small local service running. It is free while we test it, and what we want back is bug reports, not money.

Where Sidebrain is the worse choice

This is the part most comparison pages skip. Ours is shorter than theirs would be, but it is not empty.

  • It is beta. Real beta. Not "beta" as a marketing word: version 1.1.0, one developer, no support desk, no uptime promise. Sider and Monica have shipped for years, have iOS apps and answer tickets. Expect the panel to break on some site and expect to file an issue rather than get an apology email.
  • You have to install an extension, not an app. There is no Mac App Store download. You install the free Userscripts extension, allow it on the sites you want, then drop sidebrain.user.js into the folder it shows you. Three minutes if nothing goes wrong — and it goes wrong more often than a store install does.
  • Pro needs a Node service running on your Mac. Routing, memory and channel self-check live in a small local service under ~/ai-gateway. It has to be started and left running; if it dies, the panel silently drops back to the free behaviour. Competitors put that logic on their own servers, which is exactly why they don't have this problem.
  • Safari on macOS. Nothing else. No iOS, no iPad, no Chrome, no Windows, no sync between two Macs. And because Safari's extension APIs don't hand out a persistent page-docked panel, the panel is drawn inside the page by a userscript — so it does not appear on PDFs, image-only pages, or pages that block injected scripts.

Two more caveats: the subscription channel ships off by default, and whether using your ChatGPT or Claude plan through a CLI fits your agreement with them is a judgement only you can make — we invoke the CLI you installed, we never proxy or resell quota. Also, nothing here is a paid product yet, so there is no refund policy to argue about.

The bill is decided by who buys the tokens

A subscription sidebar is a reseller. You pay OpenAI, Anthropic or Google for model access, then you pay a second time for a panel that forwards your question to the same model. What that second payment buys is real — hosting, an account system, a polished UI, someone to blame when it breaks — but it is charged on a schedule, whether you asked anything that month or not, and the model underneath is the same one either way.

Sidebrain reorders it: free tiers first (Gemini, Groq, OpenRouter's free models, local Ollama), then the subscription quota you already own, and only then a pay-per-token key. When a free tier hits its quota the router cools that channel down and moves to the next one, and every answer prints which channel served it, how long it took and what it cost. That last part is the honest bit: you can watch a question cost nothing, or eleven cents, at the moment it happens, rather than at the end of a billing cycle.

Memory is the choice you can't migrate later

Switching sidebars is easy until you have six months of reading history inside one. Then the vendor's database becomes the reason you stay, which is exactly why "cloud memory" is such a good subscription feature and such a bad thing to discover after the fact. Before you commit a year, open the vendor's docs and find the sentence that says where the history is stored and whether you can export it.

Sidebrain's answer is deliberately boring: two JSONL files under ~/ai-gateway/memory/ on your Mac, one for pages, one for conversations, searchable in plain language, deletable entry by entry, with automatic snapshots of the last 20 delete and wipe operations. The trade is real — no sync between machines, no recovery if the disk dies, no web dashboard — but nobody has to trust us with it, and you can read the file with tail.

How to decide in five minutes

Three cases, in this order. Stop at the first one that describes you.

If your answer to "how much setup will I tolerate" is "none", take a hosted sidebar. Sider or Monica will be working two minutes after you sign in, with mobile apps and a support address, and their free allowance is enough to find out whether the habit sticks. Nobody should install a userscript just to discover they don't want a sidebar.

If you already pay for ChatGPT, Claude or a Gemini key, the arithmetic changes: every hosted panel asks you to buy that access a second time, every month. Sidebrain's beta exists to spend the quota you already own — free tiers first, then the plan you're logged into, then a key. It is free while we test it, and it is the roughest option on this page.

If the sensitive part is your history rather than your money, accept less convenience on purpose. A local file can be read, deleted and backed up on your terms; a cloud account cannot be closed without losing the archive inside it. Weigh that before you spend a year filling someone else's database.

It is free, and the useful thing you can give back is a bug report

The free edition is the same sidebar without routing or memory, MIT licensed. The Pro beta adds the local service and is also free to test right now — there is nothing to buy, and we would rather hear what breaks than take your card.
